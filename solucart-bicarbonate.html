Clinical Significance

Bicarbonate is not just a buffer — it's part of the prescription

Bicarbonate concentration directly affects the patient's acid-base correction during dialysis. Inconsistent powder weight, particle size or moisture content changes the final concentration — which then changes the therapy actually delivered. Consistent formulation from a trusted source means every session runs as your nephrologist prescribed.

Clinical Context

Why bicarbonate is used in dialysis

Most dialysis patients have metabolic acidosis — their blood is more acidic than it should be. The bicarbonate component of the dialysate corrects this during each session.

Corrects Acid-Base Balance

As blood passes through the dialyzer, bicarbonate moves from the dialysate into the blood — gradually correcting the chronic acidosis that builds up between sessions.

Prepares the Alkaline Component

The 720G cartridge supplies the bicarbonate side of the two-component dialysate system (the other being the acid concentrate, e.g. Acid Concentrate 5L).

Fresh, On-Demand Mixing

Bicarbonate solution is unstable when stored. Cartridge-based mixing means fresh concentrate is prepared for each session — no waiting around or bacterial growth risk.

Preparation Walkthrough

How the 720G cartridge is used

A high-level overview of how bicarbonate cartridges integrate into a hemodialysis session. Follow your facility's SOP for the exact procedure.

01

Fit the cartridge into the machine

The sealed 720G cartridge is loaded into the bicarbonate port of the hemodialysis machine, following the machine manufacturer's instructions.

02

Machine mixes the dialysate

As treatment-grade water flows through the cartridge, the bicarbonate powder dissolves, and the machine proportions it against the acid concentrate to form the final dialysate.

03

Session runs at prescribed concentration

The patient receives dialysate at the bicarbonate concentration prescribed by their nephrologist — typically between 32 and 35 mmol/L depending on clinical need.

04

Cartridge disposed of after session

Bicarbonate cartridges are single-session use. After treatment, the spent cartridge is disposed of according to your facility's clinical waste policy.

Handling & Storage

Storing bicarbonate cartridges correctly

Keep sealed until use

Bicarbonate powder absorbs moisture from the air. Do not open or puncture the seal before loading the cartridge into the machine.

Store in a cool, dry space

Avoid storage rooms with high humidity. Keep away from direct water exposure and areas prone to condensation.

Check expiry before use

Bicarbonate cartridges have a defined shelf life — inspect the date on the packaging before every session.

Stack no more than recommended

Follow the manufacturer's stacking guidance. Over-stacking can compress cartridges and damage seals before use.

Procurement FAQ

What is Solucart Bicarbonate (Bicarts) used for?

Solucart Bicarbonate 720G is a bicarbonate powder cartridge used to prepare the bicarbonate dialysate component during hemodialysis. When fitted into the machine's bicarbonate port, the powder dissolves into solution as dialysate flows through, providing the alkaline buffer that corrects metabolic acidosis in dialysis patients.

Which dialysis machines are the 720G cartridge compatible with?

The 720G cartridge is a standard size used across most modern hemodialysis machines in Kenya, including Fresenius, Nipro, B. Braun and Nikkiso systems with a bicarbonate cartridge port. If your machine uses a different cartridge size or a jug-based system, contact us — we can source the appropriate format.

How should Solucart Bicarbonate be stored?

Store in a cool, dry environment, in the original sealed packaging. Bicarbonate powder absorbs moisture — once a cartridge is opened or fitted, use it in that session. Do not use cartridges with damaged seals or past the labelled expiry date.
