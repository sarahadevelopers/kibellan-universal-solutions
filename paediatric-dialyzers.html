Specialised Paediatric Supply

Children are not small adults

Paediatric haemodialysis uses smaller circuits, lower blood flow rates, and dialyzers sized to the child's body surface area. Using adult-sized equipment in paediatric care risks haemodynamic instability and unnecessary blood loss. Kibellan stocks dedicated paediatric dialyzers for children's dialysis units across Kenya.

Clinical Context

Why paediatric sizing matters

Paediatric haemodialysis is a specialist discipline. The dialyzer choice affects haemodynamic stability, clearance efficiency, and total blood loss.

Sized to Body Surface Area

The 1.4 m² membrane matches the circulating blood volume and BSA of paediatric patients — instead of forcing a full adult dialyzer onto a small circulatory system.

Lower Priming Volume

Smaller dialyzer and lower-volume bloodline combination minimises extracorporeal blood volume — reducing the risk of hypotensive episodes during sessions.

High Flux Clearance

PES membrane with high-flux capability provides effective clearance of middle-molecular toxins — critical for children on long-term dialysis as they grow.

Common Questions

Procurement FAQ

Why do children need a different dialyzer to adults?

Children have a much lower total blood volume and body surface area than adults. Using an adult-sized dialyzer on a child can remove too much fluid, cause haemodynamic instability, and lead to significant blood loss in the extracorporeal circuit. Paediatric dialyzers have a smaller 1.4 m² membrane surface area and lower priming volume so the circuit is sized in proportion to the child's circulation.

What is the surface area of this paediatric dialyzer?

1.4 m². This is smaller than the 1.8, 2.0 and 2.1 m² dialyzers used for adults, and is designed to match the body surface area and clearance needs of paediatric patients.

What is the difference between this and a low-flux paediatric dialyzer?

This is a high-flux paediatric dialyzer with a PES membrane. High flux provides better clearance of middle-molecular toxins like beta-2-microglobulin — important for paediatric patients on long-term dialysis. Your paediatric nephrologist selects flux type based on clinical need.

Do you provide batch documentation for paediatric orders?

Yes. Every institutional order includes batch number, lot number, manufacturing date, expiry date, and the manufacturer's Certificate of Analysis where applicable. This documentation supports paediatric procurement audits and regulatory reviews.
